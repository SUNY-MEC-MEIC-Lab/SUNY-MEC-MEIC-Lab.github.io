 August 3 - 6, 2026 
 The MEIC Lab attended the 13th Korean Conference on Computer Vision (KCCV 2026), held at BEXCO in Busan from August 3 to 5. The conference gathered the domestic computer vision community around work recently presented at CVPR, ICCV, NeurIPS, and ICLR. Our group followed sessions on neural rendering and 3D scene representation, multimodal learning, and vision for robotics — topics closely tied to our ongoing work in Micro-Splatting, LiDAR-3DGS, and embodied AI. The keynote lectures and poster sessions were a useful opportunity to see how the field is moving and to exchange ideas with researchers from other labs.
We look forward to sharing our own results at KCCV in the coming years.

 February 25, 2026 
 On February 25th, MEIC Lab officially signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with WeMB (위엠비), a leading enterprise specialized in Digital Twin and AI-based integrated monitoring solutions. WeMB has been providing advanced digital transformation platforms and IT infrastructure solutions across various sectors, including smart cities, power plants, and data centers. Through this partnership, we aim to synergize MEIC Lab's cutting-edge Neural Rendering and Visual Intelligence research with WeMB's data-centric digital twin platforms. Together, we will focus on applying hyper-realistic, physics-aware 3D modeling to real-world industrial infrastructure for advanced anomaly diagnosis, predictive maintenance, and integrated monitoring. We look forward to bridging the gap between state-of-the-art computer vision research and practical industrial applications.

 September 23, 2025 
 On September 23, 2025, Prof. Jongseong Choi delivered an invited lecture at the "Nuclear & Energy Sector Workshop" hosted by the Korean Society for Noise and Vibration Engineering (KSNVE) at KAERI. Under the theme of advanced monitoring for nuclear power plants, Prof. Choi presented on "Structure Anomaly Diagnosis Technology based on Digital Twin with Hyper-realistic Visual Intelligence." (초실감 시각지능을 활용한 디지털트윈 기반 구조물 이상진단 기술) The talk highlighted MEIC Lab’s cutting-edge approach that integrates Neural Rendering with traditional structural health monitoring (SHM). By leveraging visual intelligence to create hyper-realistic digital twins, our research aims to solve critical maintenance challenges in high-risk environments like nuclear facilities. We continue to bridge the gap between computer vision and mechanical engineering to ensure the safety and reliability of critical infrastructure.
